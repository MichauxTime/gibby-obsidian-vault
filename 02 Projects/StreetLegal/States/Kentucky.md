# Kentucky — Food Truck Permit Hub

## State-Level Requirements

Every food truck operating in Kentucky must have these regardless of which city they operate in:

### 1. Kentucky Food Protection Manager Certification (CFPM)
**Required statewide.** As of summer 2025 (aligning with 2022 FDA Food Code), a Certified Food Protection Manager must be **present during all hours of operation** for any food establishment handling unpacked food — including food trucks.
- **Accepted certs:** ServSafe Manager (most common), Trust20, StateFoodSafety, or any ANSI-accredited program
- **Cost:** ~$150 (course + exam)
- **Validity:** 5 years
- **Source:** https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dph/dphps/fsb/Pages/retailfoodprogram.aspx

### 2. Commissary Requirement (State-Level)
Kentucky requires all mobile food units to operate out of a licensed commissary for food prep, water supply, ice, waste disposal, and overnight storage. This is a **state requirement**, not just a city one. Proof of commissary agreement must be submitted with any city-level MFU permit application.

**Official commissary list (updated Dec 2025):** https://www.chfs.ky.gov/agencies/dph/dphps/fsb/FSDocs/Commercial%20Kitchens%20in%20KY_12_22_25.pdf

### 3. Kentucky Sales Tax Permit
Prepared food sold in Kentucky is subject to **6% state sales tax**. No local sales taxes apply in most Kentucky cities (unlike Texas or California). Register free at revenue.ky.gov before first sale.

### 4. Kentucky Business Entity Registration
If operating as an LLC or corporation, register with the Kentucky Secretary of State via the One Stop Business Portal (onestop.ky.gov). $40 filing fee, $15 annual report. Sole proprietors may skip but lose liability protection.

### 5. Commercial Auto Insurance (State Minimum)
- Bodily injury: $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident
- Property damage: $25,000
Individual cities may require higher general liability coverage. Check city-specific MFU application requirements.

## Market Overview

Kentucky is a **emerging food truck market** at the state level. The regulatory environment is moderate — commissary requirements are strictly enforced statewide, and the 2025 update requiring a CFPM on-site during all operating hours raises the bar slightly. But permit costs remain low compared to major coastal markets.

**Lexington** is the strongest initial market: 320,000 residents, 30,000 University of Kentucky students, two Keeneland racing seasons, and a growing brewery/distillery patio scene that provides stable weekly income anchors. The Bluegrass Food Truck Association is active and advocates for operators at the city government level.

**Louisville** is the larger opportunity (633,000 city, 1.4M metro), home of the Kentucky Derby, the NuLu food and arts district, and a dense downtown with strong foot traffic. It should be the next Kentucky city researched.

**Regulatory direction:** Kentucky cities have generally modernized their food truck ordinances in the 2013–2020 wave. Lexington's 2013 pilot program was contentious but established a framework. No statewide food truck preemption law — each city sets its own zone and proximity rules on top of state health requirements.

**Seasonal considerations:** Kentucky is four-season but the outdoor vending window is roughly April–October. University towns have strong fall (football, new students) and spring (graduation season) peaks. Keeneland's April and October race meets are Kentucky-specific revenue events unique to the Lexington market.
