# Idaho — Food Truck Permit Hub

## State-Level Requirements

Every food truck operating in Idaho must obtain these state-level licenses regardless of city:

### Idaho Sales and Use Tax Permit
- **Agency:** Idaho State Tax Commission
- **Fee:** Free
- **Filing:** Online at https://tax.idaho.gov/online-services/business-registration/
- **Renewal:** One-time registration; no annual renewal required
- **Confidence:** Verified (.gov source)

### Idaho Business Registration / DBA
- **Agency:** Idaho Secretary of State
- **Fee:** $25 (assumed business name / DBA)
- **Filing:** Online at https://business.idaho.gov/register-a-business/
- **Notes:** Not required if operating as sole proprietor under your legal name
- **Confidence:** Verified (.gov source)

### Food Safety Manager Certification
- **Requirement:** At least one employee on the truck must hold a valid Food Safety Manager Certification
- **Approved providers:** ServSafe, Prometric, or other ANSI-accredited programs
- **Fee:** ~$150–$200 (exam + training)
- **Renewal:** Every 5 years
- **Enforced by:** Local county health district (e.g., Central District Health for Ada County/Boise)
- **Confidence:** Inferred (standard Idaho CDH requirement)

## Health Permit Jurisdictions

Idaho uses county-level health districts for food establishment licensing, not a single state-level agency. Key districts:

| District | Counties Covered | Key City | Contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central District Health (CDH) | Ada, Boise, Elmore, Valley | Boise | EHApps@cdh.idaho.gov / (208) 327-7499 |
| Public Health — Idaho North Central District | Several north-central counties | Moscow, Lewiston | |
| Eastern Idaho Public Health | Eastern Idaho counties | Idaho Falls, Pocatello | |

**Note:** Operators expanding beyond Boise into Meridian (Ada County — still CDH) or Nampa/Caldwell (Canyon County — Southwest District Health) need to verify district jurisdiction before assuming the same permit covers multiple cities.

## State Regulatory Landscape

Idaho is generally **business-friendly** with relatively low permit fees compared to California, Texas, or major metro states. The state-level requirements are minimal (sales tax permit + DBA), with substantive licensing handled at the county health district level.

**Key regulatory nuances:**
- No statewide food truck ordinance — regulations vary by city and county
- Downtown bans exist in Boise (BID restriction); smaller Idaho cities are generally more permissive
- Seasonal climate limits outdoor operating season (roughly April–October for most Idaho cities)
- Idaho's population boom (one of the fastest-growing states) is creating new suburban markets in the Treasure Valley

## Market Overview

Idaho's food truck market is anchored by the **Treasure Valley** (Boise, Meridian, Nampa, Eagle) — a contiguous metro of ~750,000+ people experiencing explosive growth. Boise is the economic and cultural center, hosting the state's most active food truck scene via Alive After Five concerts, Treefort Music Fest, and a thriving brewery circuit.

**Regulatory climate:** Moderate. The main friction points are Boise's downtown BID street ban (in effect since ~2002) and the county health district plan review lead time (30+ days). Fees are reasonable: the primary CDH permit is $80–$100/year, and the Boise vendor license is $141/year — significantly cheaper than California or Texas equivalents.

**Growth trajectory:** Strong. Idaho is among the top 5 fastest-growing states by percentage. New neighborhoods, office parks, and entertainment districts are opening regularly in Meridian and Eagle, creating fresh territory for food truck operators.

**SIFTA signal:** The Southern Idaho Food Truck Association's 67+ members indicate a healthy, organized industry with event pipeline and peer support — a good sign for operator sustainability.
